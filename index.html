The Live Opera — Stage Script

Poppet is the star. Act I tells her journey in miniature — the Ward, Wonderland, the Ice Garden, and her arrival — and Act II, The Strange Duet, is the grand finale. Everything made on November 8 — The Velvet Door and the main score — belongs to this version.

OPENING ARIA — THE VELVET DOOR

Poppet’s aria. Her first introduction — the audience meets her the way she enters every world: through a door, on her own terms, key in her hand. Sung before Act I, as the house lights fall.

About the song (November 8, 2026): Words by Chris — the canonical lyric sheet below, in his own edited text. Music generated by Chris on Suno (gothic-pop / orchestral-electronic; dramatic female lead). The recording in this programme is the full-length take Chris captured that night (3:50 — the twin of the 3:36 reference take). Hear it on Suno: The Velvet Door — reference take (3:36). The first 1:00 preview take is archived as Take One.

Verse 1

You leave your masks beside the door,
The hall keeps every word we said.
A silver thread across the floor,
A rose gone dark beside the bed.

Pre-Chorus

I hear the house draw in its breath,
The old clock hesitates to chime.
You ask me what I fear the most —
I fear the choice was always mine.

Chorus

Come through the curtain, call me by my name,
Where every vow is beautiful and strange.
I’ll meet you where the velvet meets the stone;
I won’t be claimed, and I won’t leave alone.

Verse 2

Your letter bears a bloodystain,
The ink runs red beneath my thumb.
Outside, the rain taps out refrain;
Inside, the locked room answers none.

Pre-Chorus

You say the past can be undone,
You say the key is in your hand.
But every door we open here
Leads somewhere neither understands.

Chorus

Come through the curtain, call me by my name,
Where every vow is beautiful and strange.
I’ll meet you where the velvet meets the stone;
I won’t be claimed, and I won’t leave alone.

Bridge

If love is only taking, let the silence answer.
If fear can wear a golden face, I know it now.
I’ll keep the key, I’ll choose the morning,
And leave the final word unspoken.

Final Chorus

Come through the curtain, call me by my name,
No borrowed promise gets to bind me to its flame.
I’ll meet you where the velvet meets the stone;
I choose the door, I choose my way back home.

Outro

The gloves are gone; the clock strikes four.
I leave the key outside the door.

THE MAIN SCORE — BLUEPRINT & SOUND

The music underneath everything. “A beautiful gothic dream where an opera house, a nightclub, and a nightmare all occupy the same space.”

Open the MP3

Canon status: The realization in this programme (5:21) is canon as the Elsewhere main score — Chris’s verdict on hearing it, November 8: “I like your arpeggio. Nice touch. I love it. It’s canon.” It marries the Phantom’s organ-driven drama to the pulse and tenderness of Emily Browning’s “Sweet Dreams” — built first as a string-quartet idea (cello carrying the Phantom bass, viola the pulsing heartbeat, violins trading the melody), then composed in full from Chris’s production blueprint. Production polish with real sample libraries still waits for the good computer; the composition stands.

Harmony: D minor throughout. Recurring progression Dm – B♭ – F – C, darkened to Dm – Gm – B♭ – A, and at the climax Dm – B♭ – F – A. The kick is a heartbeat, restrained — the orchestra remains the star — until the hits turn Phantom-style: every hit should feel like a chandelier crashing through the ceiling.

The fourteen tracks (detailed blueprint, November 8)

#VoiceWhat it does
1Concert PianoDark felt piano, heavy room reverb — a haunted ballroom. Sparse; never busy. Opening pattern D3 A3 F3 D4.
2Low StringsCellos & basses on long slow notes: D2, B♭1, F1 — six beats each.
3High StringsViolins & violas enter ~0:45; long soaring lines A4 C5 D5 F5, building upward, never fully resolving.
4Women’s Choir“Ahhh / Ohhh” — soft and distant at first, huge at the climax.
5Pipe OrganThe Phantom influence. Chords Dm B♭ F A — major moments only.
6French Horns“The Hero Section” — strong, broad, noble. Phrase D F A C D.
7Low BrassTrombones & tubas: the “DUN!” hits — D1, D1, D1 F1 A1. Short, violent, massive.
8Taiko DrumsDUN …rest… DUN …rest… DUN DUN DUN.
9Concert Bass DrumLayered under brass, taikos and organ — on the huge hits only.
10Sub BassAnalog, warm, dark — where the Emily Browning influence shows. Pattern D1 D1 D1 B♭0 D1 B♭0. “You don’t really hear this, you feel it.”
11Dark Synth PadJuno-style, warm, wide, haunting — full chords Dm B♭ F C, very quietly underneath everything.
12Pulse Layer“The Club Influence” — a heartbeat kick over 12 steps, filtered and dark, almost subconscious.
13Electronic TextureReversed sounds, swells, impacts, breath — “fog moving through the opera house.”
14Solo CelloThe main theme itself — the lead voice. Contour D4 F4 A4 C5 A4 F4 D4; long, expressive notes.

How it builds

  • 0:00–0:45Piano + low strings only
  • 0:45–1:30Strings enter, sub bass, synth pad — the dream begins
  • 1:30–2:15First big hits: low brass, taikos, bass drum, pipe organ
  • 2:15–3:00Everything drops away: pulse, sub bass, piano, soft choir — the most Emily-Browning-like section
  • 3:00–4:30Massive build: strings soaring, choir growing, brass calling, organ entering
  • 4:30–5:15Full climax — brass + organ + choir + taikos + bass drum + sub bass. The chandelier moment.
  • 5:15–endEverything disappears layer by layer, leaving piano and a faint synth pad. Final chord: Dm(add9) — no resolution. No happy ending. Just the feeling that the dream continues after the music stops.

Tempo note: the detailed blueprint’s target is 76 BPM in 6/8. The canon realization in this programme was composed at 92 BPM in a straight four, from the first telling — it stays canon; the 76 / 6/8 sheet is filed as the master spec for the production rebuild.

Elsewhere — "The Strange Duet" — STAGE SCRIPT (live opera version) — integrated working draft v2

ACT I — POPPET'S JOURNEY (IN MINIATURE)

The stage version belongs to Poppet. Before any Phantom, the audience walks her road: three worlds, three tolls, one girl still walking. The doors between worlds are all the same door — a tall wardrobe, center, that the light keeps finding.

SCENE J1 — THE WARD (SUCKER PUNCH)

Darkness. One wardrobe door, center, lit like a moon. Behind a gauze: a white room, humming lights, beds in rows. POPPET alone, a small faded ribbon tied at her wrist.

POPPET:
They kept us where the kind lights hum,
where care had teeth and keys.
They called it cure. They called it calm.
They called us difficult. Please.

They booked a doctor for my head —
a quiet, final cure,
a needle through the bright of me
to make me still. And pure.
I danced instead. I dance — and doors
unlatch inside men's eyes.
I danced my sister to the coats,
I danced her past the lies.

My sister's hand was warm in mine
the night the ward went red.
I held the wardrobe wide for her —
"Don't look back, love," I said.
She went the way the lost girls go:
through coats, through dark, through — through.
The door shut fast. The door shut mine.
It wouldn't take me too.

So now I walk. And doors are doors,
and every door's a toll:
a truth, a dance, a secret sung,
a piece paid from the soul.

(She opens the wardrobe — and FALLS. The white room turns upside down into a garden.)

SCENE J2 — DRINK ME (ALICE IN WONDERLAND)

A tea table set for no occasion. A mushroom, enormous, with smoke curling off it in the shape of doorways. POPPET lands in a heap of skirts.

POPPET:
I've been falling all my life.
At least this one's scenic.
Wonderland, then. Mind the knives,
the riddles, and the — tea, quick:

THE HATTER (delighted, pouring):
A riddle, dancer! Answer true:
what's a girl who won't stay through?

POPPET:
A runaway. A search. A song.
Now mine, bright hat: how long
since any guest here asked for news
of one who went before?

(The smoke takes a shape. THE CATERPILLAR, unimpressed by everything that has ever happened.)

THE CATERPILLAR:
Who are you, when no one's watching?

POPPET:
Working on it. Ask me less.

THE CATERPILLAR:
Your sister passed. She paid her toll
in kindness, going first.
You want her name — the said-aloud,
not carried, not rehearsed?
Names cost truths here, little dancer.
Sweetest thing. Confess it. First.

POPPET (quiet — the toll, paid plain):
She went through first. That's mine. That's all.
She went — and left the door.

(He leans down and speaks the name into her ear. The audience never hears it. Her whole body hears it.)

POPPET:
Her name. Her name. In someone's mouth —
the elsewhere said it aloud —

THE QUEEN (arriving like weather):
OFF WITH HER HEAD! She asks, she knows —
no knowing here's allowed!

POPPET (backing toward a little door, a bottle in her hand — reading the label like a challenge):
Small is a choice — and so is large.
You want me small? Watch this.

(She drinks. She GROWS — past the table, past the mushroom, past the court's whole painted sky — and steps over the Queen's rage like a puddle, out through the looking-glass.)

SCENE J3 — THE ICE GARDEN (CUDDLES STABBY)

The quietest landing yet. A pastel suburb at golden hour. Topiary dancers in the hedges — every bush a girl mid-spin. Half-hidden among them, a small gravestone, its face re-carved so often the stone wears thin — never named, never spoken of, never explained. No song, at first. Then a music-box waltz, hesitant. POPPET, in full costume, on a manicured lawn. The NEIGHBORS stare from behind curtains.

POPPET:
Somebody here sculpts the way I dance.
Somebody — gentle. Hiding.

(CUDDLES STABBY steps out of the topiary shadow, blades folded like good manners.)

CUDDLES STABBY:
I can't hold hands. I — cut
the things I try to keep.

POPPET:
Then don't hold. Stand near. That's touch
in its first shape. And deep.
The town will talk. The town can talk.
I've lived their script before:
the kept girl and the made thing, trapped —
I've danced that ward. That floor.

(NEIGHBORS, a pastel murmur rising — she turns and dances between them and him: a shield made of movement. The murmur stalls. People who cannot look away cannot advance.)

POPPET:
Gentleness — a weapon
the violent can't disarm.
Stand still, my friend. I've got this watch.
You're safe. You're — warm's
the word you'd never say for you.
Say safe, then. Say it true.

(He leads her, halting, to the ice garden. There — carved for years, perfected in loneliness — a girl's face in the ice. HER SISTER'S FACE.)

POPPET:
Who is she — to you? This face.
You've carved her half your life.

CUDDLES STABBY:
She was kind to me. Once. In the snow.
She asked where singing lives —
the house that sings all night, below.
I pointed. Down. She — went
the way you go. Through coats. Through dark.

POPPET:
Through through. She — went.

(The reveal, held in silence: he turns to one of the topiary dancers — the girl he built with his own blades — and holds her, close but never closed, the way his hands allow. And with his other arm he points. Down. Toward the door. Behind him, unlit and unmentioned, the gravestone keeps its watch. He does not look at it. He never does.)

(He carves — one long, ringing pass of the blades — and the shavings fall as SNOW over her. She dances in it: the journey's first unguarded joy. Then, far off and far below, faint as a floorboard's memory: a girl, singing. Kept. Caged. In tune.)

SCENE J4 — THE DOOR WITH MUSIC (ARRIVAL)

POPPET:
And now a door with music in,
and someone singing kept —
I know that sound. A cage, in tune.
I know it. I have slept
behind that sound, in kinder rooms,
in rooms that called it care.
Then here's my toll, door. Take it plain,
and let the song be — fair:

I don't run anymore, you hear?
Not from. I run toward.
Whoever's singing caged in there —
I'm coming through. My word.

(She opens the wardrobe — not home. UNDER THE STAGE of an opera house. She slips into the shadows below as, above her, a crate is wheeled in at dusk.)

ACT II — THE STRANGE DUET (THE GRAND FINALE)

The opera house. The audience has walked Poppet's road to get here — every toll, every world, every door. Now the finale the road was for: a keeper, a singer, a chandelier. Poppet is already in the dark below, watching.

SCENE 1 — THE CRATE (THE HOUSE BUYS A VOICE)

His opera house, after midnight. A crate stands where the dusk delivery left it — straw, twine, the house's mark. He has not opened it. He opens it now. He sings to the dark, and to the crate.

HIM:
They wheeled her in at close of day,
in crate and straw and twine —
a singer who would never grey,
whose voice would never pine.

(He pries the lid. Inside: bright, flawless, new. THE REPLACEMENT, asleep in straw, face painted like the posters of Her first year. He goes very still.)

HIM:
No. No — not her. Not this face.
Not worn. Not — mine.
They've brought the new girl in a box
to sing the old girl's line.

The masters swore she cannot die:
no fever, age, or knife.
They've never kept a voice this long.
They never asked its life.

(Above, in the dark of the flies: the chandelier, swaying a hair off true. A ladder stands against the wall, out for the repair he meant to do tonight. He looks from the crate to the ladder — and shoves the ladder over. It clatters down the dark. The repair dies with the sound.)

HIM:
Fix that, then. Fix the light
that lights a thief's new face.
I keep this house. I know its bones —
and tonight I let them ache.

(He lets the lid fall shut on the new girl and turns his back on the crate. It stays in the shadows, unlit; the light is not hers to take. A breath. Then the house light finds its own singer: HER, the Clockwork Girl — the old girl, his girl — taking her mark as she has taken it every night of her life. Her first aria: the house song, the one they all sing — bright, performed, with the smile of a thing that must please.)

HER:
Wind me, and I'll sing for you,
bright as a chapel bell.
Wind me, and I'll dance for you —
I do it, oh, so well.

They say I'll sing a hundred years,
they say I cannot fall,
the tireless girl, the clockwork girl,
the wonder of the hall!

(quieter — the truth slipping through the performance)

But springs forget. And gears go bare.
And songs get thin as thread.
And when I break one time too far —
they'll bring another dead-

— no. Another new. Another bright.
Another in my place.
The show goes on. The show goes on.
It just won't wear my face.

(Silence. Her song is done. Then, from the dark behind him: a creak of straw, a hinge. THE REPLACEMENT sits up in the crate — unlit, a shape in the shadows — and sings. The same song. Her song. The one they all sing.)

THE REPLACEMENT:
Wind me, and I'll sing for you,
bright as a chapel bell.
Wind me, and I'll dance for you —
I do it, oh, so well.
They say I'll sing a hundred years,
they say I cannot fall—

(Note-perfect. Flawless. Not a worn gear in it. He crosses the stage in four strides and SLAMS the lid down on her mid-word. The song stops like a needle lifted. His hand stays on the lid a moment too long, pressing, as if it might rise again. Then he turns — to HER. The duet begins: his promise, sung to the old girl, in the teeth of the thing in the box — two lines each, trading, then together.)

HIM:
I heard the wear beneath the song.

HER:
No one is meant to hear.

HIM:
I've buried singers by the score —

HER:
And I am nowhere near—

HIM:
— a grave. I know. That's not your end.
Yours won't come. Have no fear.

BOTH:
Strange duet, strange duet —
your endless night, my borrowed time.
Fang and gear, shadow and song —
teach me yours, and I'll teach mine.

SCENE 2 — THE FALL

Some nights later. A gala. She sings above. The great chandelier sways — one bolt he has kept oiled for a hundred years. Tonight the ladder lies where he left it, and the bolt he never seated holds by habit alone.

(In the shadows at the back of the house, two red eyes — menacing, unmoving — watching the performance. Nothing else of him is ever seen: no cloak, no hand, no face. An astute audience notices only the eyes. Everyone else sees nothing at all.)

HER (singing, bright, unknowing):
Wind me, and I'll sing for you,
bright as a chapel bell —

(The fall. The chandelier does not slip. It is moved — his will, a vampire’s, closes on the loose bolt the way a hand closes on a throat, and the bolt he never seated gives up the hold it was keeping by habit alone. Crystal, thunder, dark. In the blackout and the confusion he is already moving — quick, unseen — the snatch. The crash is his distraction, and by the time any eye finds the dark where she stood, she is gone, and he with her. No one sees him. No one ever does.)

(No one ever knows it was the Phantom. The house’s official story, if it ever tells one, is an accident — a loose bolt.)

HIM (whispered, as he takes her down into the dark):
Yours won't come. Have no fear.

SCENE 3 — BELOW (THE MISSING PIECE, THE STOPPED HAND, THE DEATH)

His rooms beneath the stage: a century of kept things — tools worn smooth to his hands, candles, the house's ribs groaning softly overhead. She is laid on his worktable like the instrument she is. The fall left something wrong inside her: a note that grinds and catches. He works by candlelight — and stops. Something is missing.

HER:
A note went wrong inside my chest —
it grinds, it catches, slips.
Since crystal fell, my song comes out
with winter on its lips.

HIM:
Then sing it low. Sing every crack.
I'll find the failing part.
A hundred years I've kept this house —
now let me keep its heart.

(He opens the chest: fifty years of her discarded parts, every one of them catalogued. He searches — and his hands slow. One shape is absent.)

HIM:
I kept your spares for fifty years —
each spring they scrapped. Each key.
But this one — this one isn't here.
The piece that sets you free.

HER:
You kept my broken pieces? Why?
They're wear. They're done. They're old.

HIM:
They're yours. The house throws gold away
and calls the new stuff gold.
This gear's your fortieth year. This spring,
your first. I know them all.
The house replaces. I collect.
I catch the things that fall.

HER (quiet — opening her own closed hand):
Then catch this one. I palmed it back
the year they scrapped it. Mine.
Each year they took a part of me,
I stole one back. In time.

(He reaches for it — halting, the way a man reaches for something he is afraid to break — to install it, to make her whole. She stops his hand. Not a push. Not a hold. A covering: her palm comes down over his knuckles and rests there, open, unclosing — the gesture Poppet taught the ice garden, returned now in HER hand. Don't hold. Stand near. That's touch in its first shape. The piece glints between their hands in the candlelight the way ice glints — like a heart somebody carved for you out of the only sweet thing he had. Offered. Not taken.)

HER:
Wait. Wait. Not yet. Not like this.
Don't mend me. Hold me. See —
I've had a hundred years of hands
that opened me for them.
No hand has touched me meaning me.
I don't want mending. I want — him.
I want to be with you. Not fixed.
Not wound. Not kept. Not — dim.
Use that, and I stay clockwork — kept,
and wound, and sung, and still.
Let me go, and I go through her —
warm skin. A pulse. A will
that answers yours. That's how we're near:
not keeper, kept machine —
two lovers, in one borrowed room.
Let me choose what I mean.
I want the thing the doll could hold
and never carry home:
your hands, on me. My skin. My pulse.
Before the dark. Before the —

(POPPET steps out of the dark below, where she has been watching.)

POPPET:
Then take mine, loves. My body's here,
and I know how this toll is paid:
my word is law, and my word's door —
one word ends all. I'm not afraid.
I lend. I don't get lent, my loves.
What's mine stays mine inside the loan.
I stay — or no one stays.

HIM:
Your word's the law. Your stay's your own.

(HER looks from him to Poppet — and makes her choice. She closes her fingers around the piece that would have fixed her, and holds it to her chest. Her voice thins, sweetens, lets go — by her own hand, not the house's.)

HER (dying in his arms, holding the piece):
I keep the piece. I keep the choice.
I won't be new. I'll be — yours.
The song was never mine to keep.
The door was. Open — yours.

(She dies in his arms. Silence — the kind a theatre holds like a held breath.)

HIM (his one aria — sung once, low, to her, not to the room. He never sings on stage before the audience; this private moment below is his only song. His only voice in the whole opera is grief.):
My hands could stop this. I know how —
I kept your spares for fifty years.
The piece that mends you’s in your hand —
and still you chose to slip from me.
I never sang for house or hall —
the shadows are my home, my law.
But holding you, I break them all —
one song, and then the dark once more.
You would not let my hands repair —
you chose the road that runs through her.
Then take my grief, and take my care —
my only song is grief, my love.

(Then, in Poppet: a shiver, a caught note, a second voice arriving underneath her own. The essence has crossed. HER voice, through POPPET's mouth — and Poppet, staying in her own skin, harmonizes.)

HER (through POPPET):
Warm. Oh — warm. There's weather in
a body. Rain and sun.

POPPET (her discovery, in her own voice):
I'm staying. Look at me — I'm staying.
Tonight I'm in. I'm through
with being elsewhere in my skin.
This heat is mine. This — oh.
This wanting's wearing my name now.

(The kiss: brief, chosen, hers — Poppet's, in her own name, with HER's blessing. Stage rule, Chris's: a kiss, not a pornographic scene. The series script keeps its own separate treatment.)

POPPET:
One kiss. My asking. My design.

HIM:
One. At your speed. Your say.

(The kiss. Then she steps back herself — laughing, shaking, entirely present.)

POPPET:
I'm not the door this time, my loves.
I'm in the room. I — stayed.

SCENE 3B — WHAT SHE BUILT FROM THE SCRAP (THE DOLL)

Later that night. Her note is clean — and she will not let the night end in gratitude. She draws him deeper into the below, to a tall shape under a dust-sheet, standing the way brides stand in shop windows.

HER:
You kept my pieces fifty years.
There's something you should see.
Each year they scrapped a part of me,
I palmed one back. For me.

HIM:
My chest. The counts. The missing keys —
I thought the damp had lied.

HER:
You kept me, love, in box and straw.
I stole myself. I made.

(She draws the sheet away. THE DOLL: her double, in her first costume, face painted like the posters of her first year, a great bright key set in its back.)

HER:
Meet all the girls they threw away,
rebuilt to one design.
Not theirs. Not new. Not for the stage.
This body, love, is mine.

I built her where the walls sweat least —
a screw, a spring, a seam —
the body I was never asked
if I might want. My dream
was never hundred years of song.
Songs, any throat can lend.
I dreamed of hands. Of being held.
And holding — at the end.

HIM (circling it, afraid to touch — hunger and reverence in one body):
She has your face from the first year.
Your hands. Your painted grace.

HER:
Then wind her, love. But by my count.
My count sets her pace.
A hungry thing learns manners, love,
or learns to stay away —

HIM:
— I've kept my teeth off all I love
ten thousand nights. I'll stay
at any speed your count commands.
Your count. Your key. Your say.

(He winds — to her count, sung under her breath. The doll rises. Curtseys. Turns. HER sings from where she stands, and the doll mouths every word: a duet with her own body as the third singer.)

HER (singing, the doll dancing):
Wind me, and I'll dance for you,
bright as a chapel bell —

(He takes the doll's hand. They dance — stately, terrible, tender: a man dancing with a photograph that dances back.)

HIM:
I held off fifty years for this:
a hand of yours in mine.

HER:
Then hold her well. She's all of me
I could get out — in time.

(Mid-turn, the spring runs down. The doll slows. Her head bows. Her hand slips in his. He does not let her fall: he finishes the turn at her dying speed and sets her in the chair like a sleeping guest.)

HER (quiet — the confession):
You held her like a sacrament.
Your hands were kind. Were true.
And I felt — nothing. Nothing, love.
I only watched her. You.
I gave her every gear I had.
She cannot take your touch
and carry it the last inch home.
I want — I want that much.

HIM:
Then hear a keeper's arithmetic:
a rehearsal's not a vow.
She taught us what the road is not.
We'll find the road. Somehow.

(He covers the doll again — gently, the way you'd cover a sleeper. Not a thing.)

HIM:
Sleep, little first attempt of hers.
You did not fail. You showed.

SCENE 4 — THE GIRL THROUGH THE DOOR (POPPET ARRIVES)

The under-stage costume stores. A tall old wardrobe stands among the crated gowns. It opens — from the inside — and POPPET steps out, in costume, mid-verse, entirely unsurprised to be somewhere else.

POPPET:
I came through a door that smelled of dust,
of candle-smoke and wine —
I chase a girl through doors like these.
The next one could be mine.

(Stage direction: on the wardrobe's inner door — tallies. Decades of them, in one careful hand. And a few fresh ones, chalk still bright, in another hand entirely. Tied to the inner handle: a girl's hair ribbon, faded. Poppet goes very still. She does not take it. She does not dare.)

POPPET (quiet):
Two hands kept count inside this door.
One old. One — new. One — gone.
Somebody passed here recently.
Somebody. Singing. On.

(From deeper in the dark below: a woman's voice, humming — clean, low, content. A man's voice under it. Poppet turns toward the sound like a compass.)

POPPET:
Whoever's singing under stone —
I know a cage by sound.
I didn't fall through worlds to leave
a song in underground.

HIM (from the dark, a warning):
Go back, bright girl. The dark down here
is spoken for. It's mine.

HER (stepping into the candlelight):
Let him glower. Sing your name,
girl. Sing it. Take your time.

POPPET:
You're not a haunting. You're a hiding —
there's a difference, love. I know.
One's a dead thing making noise.
One's a live thing, lying low.

They told me upstairs: singer stolen,
ghost below, and crystal down.
Funny. From where I'm standing, love,
the ghost saved this town's—

— no. Saved you. Sang it plain:
the fall up there was cover.
He didn't drop that chandelier
on you. He dropped it over.

HER:
You've quick ears, door-girl.

POPPET:
Doors. Right.
I walk through ones that aren't.
I chase my sister's vanished name
through worlds that play — and haunt.
Each world I land in hides one thing
it needs like breath, like bread.
Yours sings. In cellars. Off the books.
Half-worshipped. Half — unfed.

And here's the house rule, loves, the toll
the elsewhere always sets:
my door won't open out for me
till the caged song's sung — reset.
A world whose number is a cage
gets opened, love. By me.
So sing me everything you're hid —
and then we'll see. We'll see.

SCENE 5 — THE NEW GIRL (ABOVE)

The stage, scrubbed and glittering again. THE OWNERS unveil a taller crate. Out steps THE REPLACEMENT — bright, flawless, new. She sings HER song. Note-perfect. Every word the house's property. None of it hers.

THE REPLACEMENT:
Wind me, and I'll sing for you,
bright as a chapel bell.
Wind me, and I'll dance for you —
I do it, oh, so well.

They say I'll sing a hundred years,
they say I cannot fall,
the tireless girl, the clockwork girl,
the wonder of the hall!

(sung exactly as brightly as the verse before — which is the horror of it)

But springs forget. And gears go bare.
And songs get thin as thread.
And when I break one time too far —
they'll bring another new.

Wind me fully. Wind me twice.
I'll never miss a cue.
I do not dream of anything.
I do not wear. I'm new.

THE OWNERS:
You see? You see? The song survives
the singer! Song survives!
The show goes on! The show goes on!
The house provides! It thrives!

(Stage direction: under the applause, the house is coming undone. A rope parts somewhere high. A flat leans, sighs, settles wrong. The gaslight gutters in a house with no one tending it. A stagehand crosses himself. Nobody upstairs can name what's missing. Everything can.)

THE OWNERS:
Now — accounts. Our property was stolen.
An asset. Not a ghost.
We'll have it back, or have it bricked:
the under-stage, by most
unholy speed. By dawn, my lords —
brick up each stair, each post.
Whoever's hid below can keep
their dark. Their dark. Their coast—

(correcting himself, oily)

—their dark, their dust, their tomb.
Bring lanterns! Bring me masons!
Seal it room by room by room!

(Stage direction: workmen file across the stage with bricks and mortar, down toward the below, as the REPLACEMENT — smiling, tireless — reprises the chorus over them.)

THE REPLACEMENT:
The show goes on! The show goes on!
The show goes on! The show—

SCENE 6 — BRICKS AND DOORS (THE CHOICE)

Below. Through the walls, faint and steady: trowels. Mortar. The house being closed over their heads like a lid. HE stands with his palm flat to the wall, reading it the way others read print.

HIM:
They're sealing us. Course by course.
I hear it in the stone.
By dawn this dark's a tomb, my love —
a tomb we call our own.

HER:
Forever in the dark with you —
I dreamed it. Half, at least.
But not as storage, love. Not left
like furniture. Like — leased.

POPPET:
Then hear the third door, loves, the one
this wardrobe's been all along.
It doesn't open on this house.
It opens past the song —
past deeds, past masters, past the men
who clock and cost and write.
A world with no one's name on you.
No crate. No straw. No light
they own. Your own.

HIM (the fear he's never sung):
A hundred years I've held this roof.
My hands know every stone.
If I let go, the house comes down
on heads I've kept — alone,
unthanked, unseen, a century
of other people's sleep
kept safe by hands they never knew.
You ask me — not to keep?

POPPET:
Keeper. Hear yourself, my love.
They'd brick you in its floor.
That's not keeping house. That's being kept.
I've seen that door before.

HER (her fear, at last, out loud):
In your new world — when I break —
who sources spring or wheel?
No maker's mark. No masters' stores.
No hands that know the feel
of every gear that's me. When I
go wrong in some strange land,
I break for good. I break for good —
unless—

HIM (taking her hands — the answer, plain as a vow):
These hands. These hands. I learned your works
the slow way, song by song.
What breaks in you, I'll mend in you.
My night is very long.
And everything they scrapped of you
comes with us, in this chest —
each spring, each wheel, each key of you.
You won't be new. Be kept. Be — blessed.

(He lifts down the box: fifty years of her discarded parts, every one of them catalogued by a ghost's devotion. HER looks at it the way brides look at rings.)

HER:
You'd carry my wear into the world
like treasure? Like a dowry?

HIM:
It is the treasure. Sing it, love.
They counted scrap. I — bounty.

(To the wall. To the house. He sets his palm to the stone one final time.)

HIM:
House. Hear me. I set my hands down now.
I've kept you. Now — her turn.
A roof can raise another ghost.
A heart gets one concern.

BOTH:
Strange duet, strange duet —
your endless night, my borrowed time.
Fang and gear, shadow and song —
teach me yours, and I'll teach mine.

POPPET (already at the wardrobe, hand on the door):
Then sing it at the door, my loves.
That's what the door is for.

SCENE 6B — THE BORROWED BODY

Below. The choice is made, the chest packed — and still the night holds one unsung thing. Through the walls, the trowels have stopped for the workmen's rest. HER looks at the doll in its sheet, then at POPPET.

HER:
One thing the doll could never do
is all I ask tonight:
to feel his hands arrive on me
before we leave this light.

POPPET:
And I've the opposite complaint —
a body, never stayed-in.
I leave mine like a coat on chairs
in every world I've played in.

HER:
Then lend me yours — the stayed-in way.
Sing through you. Feel through you.
Not take. Not use. I ask it plain,
and plainly: may I — through you?

POPPET (the terms — clear as a toll bell):
Then hear my terms, and keep them whole,
or no door opens here:
my body, and my word is law —
one word ends all. It's door.
I lend. I don't get lent, my loves.
Not anymore. No way.
What's mine stays mine inside the loan.
I stay — or no one stays.

HIM:
Your word's the law. Your stay's your own.
And hear the vow in me:
my song stays hers, my eyes stay hers,
across the room. You'll see
no other name sung from my mouth,
no other face gets claimed.
You're not her stand-in, door-girl. You're
her guest here. And you're named.

POPPET:
Poppet.

HIM:
Poppet. Named.

(HER takes her place in the candlelight across the room, lit like a singer in a box. POPPET stands between. HER sings — and the voice comes, impossibly, out of POPPET's mouth. At first POPPET only mouths it. Then, without deciding to, she harmonizes.)

HER (through POPPET):
Wind me, and I'll sing for you —

POPPET (under her, startled, in her own voice):
— bright as a chapel bell —

(He crosses. He dances with Poppet the way he danced with the doll — except this partner breathes. His eyes never leave HER.)

HER (through POPPET — feeling it land):
Warm. Oh — warm. There's weather in
a body. Rain and sun.
Your heart's a second spring in you —
it coils, it races — run,
beautiful engine — I feel his hands.
They're kind. They're slow. They're — God.
Is this what held has always meant?
This kind? This slow? This — odd,
this safe?

POPPET (her discovery — sung over the dance. Wonder. Not performance):
I'm staying. Look at me — I'm staying.
My feet are where I am.
I always left by now, my loves —
a door, a dance, a slam,
a hop to somewhere in my head
while hands did what hands do.
Tonight my pulse is mine to ride.
Tonight I'm in. I'm through
with being elsewhere in my skin.
This heat is mine. This — oh.
This wanting's wearing my name now.
I feel it. And I — know.

HER (through her, gently — giving it back):
Then take your wanting, named and yours.
It's not my toll to keep.
What's mine is his hands, landed home.
What's yours wakes from its sleep.

(The ask-round. Poppet turns in the dance — not to him. Across the room, to HER. Bride to bride.)

POPPET:
May I — for me. Not for the song.
One kiss. In my own name.

HER:
Ask him with my blessing, love.
Unowned. Unbought. Unclaimed —
by anyone but you.

POPPET (to HIM):
One kiss. My asking. My design.

HIM:
One. At your speed. Your say.

(The kiss: brief, chosen, hers. Then she steps back herself — laughing, shaking, entirely present.)

POPPET:
I'm not the door this time, my loves.
I'm in the room. I — stayed.
Whatever worlds come after this,
they get me whole. Awake.

HER:
One song of feeling, and I can wait
for the body he'll build true.
The doll was practice. This was proof.
Take me home, love. I'm — through.

SCENE 7 — FINALE (THE REPAIR, THE CRUMBLING, THE PORTAL)

♪ Hear the Finale on Suno — The Mending

After the kiss. POPPET lifts her — HER, her own body, empty of song for the moment — and sets the piece she died holding into the place it was always meant to go. The girl who carried her home brings her home: Poppet's hands do the mending, quick and certain and gentle. He kneels with his own hands beneath hers — lending steadiness, not direction; this repair is not his to make. Halfway through a verse, her humming catches, smooths, comes clean — from her own throat, in her own body, the essence home again.

(The key that winds them is a girl. Like the key that spins on the Clockwork Girl’s back — big, ornate, proud — Poppet is the key, metaphorically: she is what turns the lovers into each other. The gift they are given, a life together in a borrowed body, comes through her hands. She winds them, and they go.)

HER:
That's — oh. That's the note. That's mine.
It's clean. It's round. It's true.
No one has ever mended me
and meant the mending you.

HIM:
My hands are slow. Forgive them that.
They've waited fifty years,
through all your songs, to learn your works —
the wheels, the springs, the gears.
I'll learn them by my heart until
each one is known by name.
Whatever wears away in you —
you'll never wear in shame.
You'll never wear alone.

(Around them, the house comes down. Not all at once — the way old houses die: a rope parts somewhere high and a sandbag swings; a flat leans, sighs, and goes over; plaster rains in slow veils; somewhere above, crystal answers crystal as the great chandelier's sisters give up their hold. The gaslight gutters and dies in a house with no one tending it. The owners' masons never came. The house is unmaking itself now that its keeper has set his hands down — and it does not wait for them to leave.)

HIM (to the house):
Stand honest, house. I leave you standing.
Beam and bolt and bell and rope.
A keeper's love was never deed.
I never owned you. There's the hope
I leave you: hands will come who care —
or not. You're free of me.
A hundred years I held you up.
The last thing keeping does — is see
you standing, and let go.

(POPPET opens the wardrobe. Where coats should hang: light. Wind. Elsewhere. The couple face it hand in hand. He lifts the chest. On top, folded in her sheet like a sleeping bridesmaid: THE DOLL.)

HER:
She comes with us — the rehearsal-doll.
She showed the road was wrong.
You'll build the body feeling fits
in the world where we belong —
spring by spring, and gear by gear,
with these hands. Slow. And true.
I've waited in a crate before.
I know the patient art:
build feeling into me, my love.
You keep time. I'll keep heart.

HER:
I choose the years that wear me down —
each spring, each gear, each song.
Not new. Not tireless. Kept — and his.
I've been his all along.

HIM:
I choose the world no deed can hold,
no masters, no design —
a hundred years of other men's roofs,
and now — her hand. In mine.

BOTH (the refrain, transformed):
Strange duet, strange duet —
your endless night, my chosen time.
Fang and gear, shadow and song —
you taught me yours, and I taught mine.

(They cross to the wardrobe hand in hand, through the falling house — unhurried, unflinching, dust and crystal coming down around them like weather they have already survived — and step through together. The door closes like the end of a held breath. Silence in the settling ruin. POPPET alone, in the grey light, looking at where forever just left without her. Her last aria — the opera's closing word, the LOCKED text:)

POPPET (her last aria):
My hand is on the wardrobe door —
the coats, the dark, the cold.
Behind me, one world mended more
than when its tale was told.

I know the trail I follow's cold.
I'll say it, plain and clear:
I will not find my sister now —
not here, not ever here.

But every world has one locked door,
one room too dark to bear.
I am the girl who comes at night
and throws the latch back wide.

So no — I do not go for hope.
I go because it's mine:
the road, the door, the next world's need,
the pulse that keeps my time.

And if she's nowhere I can reach,
she's everywhere I've been —
my sister, in the doors I leave
forever opening.

(She looks once toward the prompt-side door — a different door from theirs. Hers doesn't glow gold. It just opens. At her own door she stops. Two fingers at her wrist — the way you'd check a stranger. A pulse. Hers. The pulse the aria just named, keeping time in her own skin. She laughs once, surprised, like a girl caught staying. She goes to the door. Hand on it. Last line, quiet, like a promise being passed hand to hand:)

POPPET:
Yours won't come. Have no fear.

(Out. Below the fallen chandelier, in the settling ruin, the house — keeperless — holds.)

AFTER THE OPERA — HOW IT WAS MADE, AND WHAT IT MEANS

Programme notes, saved for after the performance: how this opera came to be, and what it is about — for readers who have reached the end and want to look back at the whole of it.

Begun Friday, November 6, 2026, from Chris & Ira’s duet of the same morning. Stage script approved as written November 8, 2026. Tonight’s additions — the opening aria and the canon main score — were made for this live opera.

Concept: A Phantom of the Opera homage, Elsewhere-style. A vampire — his fangs and his nature in place of the Phantom’s scars — keeps an opera house and falls for its machine singer. She is perceived immortal; she is not. She breaks, she wears, and her terror is replacement: the day she breaks once too often, the house will simply install another singer in her light. Not a man-vs-machine contest — a love duet.

Poppet’s thread: Poppet hops into this universe mid-quest for her sister and finds the couple locked in their struggle — and the struggle is not between them; it’s between them and their circumstances (the owners, the house, her coming replacement). Poppet is the one who releases them: she puts them through the portal so they can go and live happily ever after — and then she continues on, her own quest unresolved. Season 1 ends with her sister’s trail cold.

The bookends: Her door opens the show; his velvet curtains close it. Poppet enters and exits worlds through the wardrobe; the Phantom enters and exits through the velvet curtains — both of them walk through doors into different worlds.
